Contents: statutory rights a contract cannot exclude, rights that can be excluded only between trading parties, and the check to run before accepting a disclaimer clause.

## Cannot be excluded at all

- **Fair Trading Act 1986, s 5C** - no contracting out of the Act's protections is permitted by a term that purports to exclude, restrict or modify the Act's application, or any right or remedy under it, except as s 5D allows (see below). A clause saying "the supplier is not liable for any misleading statement" does not work against s 9 (misleading and deceptive conduct) or s 13 (false representations).
- **Privacy Act 2020** - the Information Privacy Principles (s 22) cannot be contracted out of by a business towards the individuals whose personal information it holds; an individual cannot sign away their rights under the Act by agreeing to a privacy policy that conflicts with it.
- **Consumer Guarantees Act 1993, where the buyer acquires goods or services for personal, domestic or household use** - the guarantees in Part 1 and Part 4 cannot be excluded, restricted or modified (s 43(1)), subject only to the trade exception below.

## Can be excluded only between parties in trade, and only if done correctly

- **Fair Trading Act 1986, s 5D** - parties who are both "in trade" can contract out of specified provisions, but only if the contract is in writing, and it is fair and reasonable for the parties to be bound by that term, considering factors such as bargaining power, whether the parties had independent advice, and industry custom.
- **Consumer Guarantees Act 1993, s 43(2)** - the guarantees can be contracted out of if the goods or services are acquired for business purposes (not personal, domestic or household use) and the contract is in writing and it is fair and reasonable to allow it.
- **Fair Trading Act 1986, unfair contract terms (ss 26A, 26B, 46H-46M)** - these apply to standard form consumer contracts and, since 16 August 2022, to standard form "specified trade contracts" below the prescribed annual value threshold; a term cannot be rescued from being unfair merely by labelling it a trade contract if it falls inside these provisions.

## The check before accepting a disclaimer, limitation or waiver clause

1. Identify which Act's right the clause is trying to limit or exclude.
2. Check whether that Act allows contracting out at all for this situation.
3. If it does, check the specific conditions (writing, both parties in trade, fair and reasonable) are actually met by the drafting, not just asserted by a recital.
4. If the clause fails this check, flag it as unenforceable against that right, cite the section, and propose wording that achieves the client's commercial intent without the unenforceable exclusion (for example, a liability cap instead of a full exclusion, where capping is itself permitted).
