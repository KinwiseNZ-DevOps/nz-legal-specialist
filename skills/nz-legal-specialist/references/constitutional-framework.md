## The correction to make

New Zealand has no single written, codified constitution. There is no one document called "the New Zealand Constitution" for wording to be checked against. A request to make wording "abide by the NZ Constitution" needs translating into which actual source applies, and usually the answer for a commercial contract, terms of trade, or privacy policy is: none of these directly govern private contract wording, but they shape the legal system the contract sits inside.

## The real sources, in practice

- **Constitution Act 1986** - sets out the structure of government (the Sovereign, Parliament, the Executive, the judiciary). Rarely bears directly on a commercial contract, but confirms Parliament's supremacy: an Act of Parliament (like the Fair Trading Act or Privacy Act) overrides conflicting contract terms.
- **New Zealand Bill of Rights Act 1990 (NZBORA)** - protects rights against the state, not generally between private parties, but relevant if a contract involves a public-sector counterparty, or a clause that would require unlawful discrimination (see also the Human Rights Act 1993, which does apply to private conduct in employment, provision of goods and services, and more).
- **Senior Courts Act 2016 and the ordinary courts** - the judicial branch that interprets and enforces both statute and contracts.
- **Te Tiriti o Waitangi / the Treaty of Waitangi (1840)** - not itself part of domestic law unless a specific Act gives it effect, but referenced in many statutes' principles clauses and relevant to government and some commercial dealings with Māori entities, land, or taonga. Treat any Treaty-related clause as a specialist topic requiring its own legal advice; this skill does not give Treaty-clause sign-off.
- **Statute and the common law** - for commercial drafting, the law that actually applies is specific: the Contract and Commercial Law Act 2017 (general contract rules), the Fair Trading Act 1986 and Consumer Guarantees Act 1993 (trading and consumer protection), the Privacy Act 2020 (personal information), the Commerce Act 1986 (competition), and the common law of contract built up through court decisions.

## What this means for drafting

When a user asks for "constitutionally correct" wording, ask what they actually mean: usually one of (a) wording that will hold up in a New Zealand court under ordinary contract and statute law, (b) wording that does not breach a specific Act's non-excludable protections, or (c) wording that respects Treaty principles in a specific regulated context. Route to the right source rather than citing "the Constitution" as if it were one rulebook.
