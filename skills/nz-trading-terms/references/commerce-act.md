Contents: anti-competitive arrangements, misuse of market power, resale price maintenance, relevance to trading terms.

Source: Commerce Act 1986, legislation.govt.nz. Enforced by the Commerce Commission. This is competition law, relevant to trading terms mainly for exclusivity, most-favoured-customer, resale price and market-allocation clauses between businesses. Verify current section numbers before citing.

## Restrictive trade practices (Part 2)

- **s 27** - a person must not enter into a contract, arrangement or understanding, or give effect to a provision in one, that has the purpose, effect or likely effect of substantially lessening competition in a market. This covers exclusivity clauses, territory or customer allocation, and information-sharing arrangements between competitors where the effect is significant.
- **s 36** - misuse of market power: a person with a substantial degree of market power must not engage in conduct for the purpose of excluding or deterring a competitor, preventing or deterring competitive conduct, or eliminating a competitor. Relevant to exclusive dealing, tying, and refusal-to-supply clauses drafted by a dominant supplier.
- **s 37 / s 38** - resale price maintenance: a supplier must not require or induce a reseller to sell at or above a specified minimum price. A clause requiring a distributor or reseller to maintain a set resale price is at risk under these sections; recommending or publishing a price (**s 39**) is generally permitted if it is clearly non-binding.

## Drafting checks for trading terms

- An exclusivity clause, minimum purchase requirement, or territory restriction in a distribution or supply agreement should be assessed for its actual effect on competition in the relevant market, not just its label; flag for specialist competition law review where the parties have meaningful market share, rather than giving a definitive clearance.
- A "minimum advertised price" or "minimum resale price" clause should be checked against ss 37-38; a true "recommended retail price" that the reseller remains free to depart from is lower risk, but wording that pressures or requires adherence is not.
