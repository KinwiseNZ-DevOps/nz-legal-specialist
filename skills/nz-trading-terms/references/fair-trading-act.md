Contents: misleading and deceptive conduct, unsubstantiated representations, false representations, unfair practices, unfair contract terms, enforcement.

Source: Fair Trading Act 1986 (FTA), legislation.govt.nz. Administered by MBIE / enforced by the Commerce Commission. Verify current section numbers before citing.

## Misleading and deceptive conduct (ss 9-14A)

- **s 9** - misleading and deceptive conduct generally: no person in trade shall engage in conduct that is misleading or deceptive, or likely to mislead or deceive. Intent is irrelevant; the test is the likely effect on the audience, including the overall impression (fine print cannot cure a misleading headline claim).
- **s 10** - misleading conduct in relation to goods (nature, manufacturing process, characteristics, suitability, quantity).
- **s 11** - misleading conduct in relation to services (nature, characteristics, suitability, quantity).
- **s 12** - misleading conduct in relation to employment (terms, conditions, availability).
- **s 13** - false or misleading representations: a non-exhaustive list including false representations about quality, standard, grade, composition, price, sponsorship or approval.
- **s 14** - false representations and other misleading conduct in relation to land.

## Unsubstantiated representations (ss 12A-12D)

- **s 12A** - a trader must not make an unsubstantiated representation: one made without reasonable grounds, regardless of whether it later turns out to be true. Obvious exaggeration ("puffery") that no reasonable person would take literally is not caught.
- **s 12B** - the court must have regard to when the representation was made, what grounds existed at that time, and whether those grounds were reasonable.
- Drafting check: before a marketing claim, benefit statement, or comparison goes into terms of trade or promotional copy, confirm the client can point to the actual evidence supporting it, dated to when the claim is made.

## Unfair practices (ss 17-26)

- **s 19** - bait advertising (advertising goods/services the trader does not have reasonable grounds to believe can be supplied as advertised).
- **s 20** - referral selling (inducing a purchase with a promise of a benefit for finding other customers).
- **s 21** - demanding or accepting payment without intending to supply as ordered.
- **s 21A-21C** - a recipient of unsolicited goods or services is not liable to pay for them, and a trader must not assert a right to payment for them.
- **s 22** - misleading representations about certain business activities (e.g. work-from-home or investment opportunities).
- **s 23** - harassment and coercion.
- **s 24** - pyramid selling schemes prohibited; **s 40A** adds an additional penalty where the offence involved commercial gain.

## Unfair contract terms (ss 26A, 26B, 26C-26E, 46H-46M)

- **s 26A** - unfair contract terms in standard form consumer contracts.
- **s 26B** - unfair contract terms in standard form trade contracts, where the contract meets the "specified trade contract" definition in **s 26C-26D** (a trading relationship below a prescribed annual value threshold).
- **s 46H** - application for a declaration that a term is an unfair contract term is made to the District Court (the Commerce Commission brings most applications).
- **s 46I** - the court's power to declare a term unfair.
- **s 46J** - what makes a contract a "standard form" contract (offered on a take-it-or-leave-it basis, with no real opportunity to negotiate).
- **s 46K** - terms that may not be declared unfair: terms defining the main subject matter, setting the upfront price, or required or expressly permitted by another enactment.
- **s 46L** - the test: a term is unfair if it would cause a significant imbalance in the parties' rights and obligations, it is not reasonably necessary to protect the legitimate interests of the party advantaged by it, and it would cause detriment (financial or otherwise) to a party if relied on. The whole contract and the term's transparency are considered.
- **s 46M** - examples of terms that may be unfair: a term letting one party but not the other avoid or limit its obligations, terminate the contract, vary the terms, or penalise one party for breach or termination while not penalising the other equivalently.
- Drafting check: a standard-form term giving only the drafting party a unilateral right to vary the contract, price, or specifications, with no equivalent right or real limit, is a common unfair-term risk; flag it and propose mutual or constrained wording (e.g. variation only on notice, with a right to terminate instead of being bound).

## Enforcement (Part 5)

- **s 40** - contravening Parts 1 to 4A is an offence.
- **s 41** - the court may grant an injunction.
- **s 43** - the court may make other orders (e.g. varying the contract, refunds) on application under **s 43A**, subject to the limits on District Court and Disputes Tribunal jurisdiction in **s 43B**.
- **s 44** - defences (e.g. reasonable mistake, reliance on information from another person, reasonable precautions and due diligence).
- Penalties and specific dollar thresholds change by amendment; verify the current maximum penalty figures on legislation.govt.nz rather than quoting a remembered number.
