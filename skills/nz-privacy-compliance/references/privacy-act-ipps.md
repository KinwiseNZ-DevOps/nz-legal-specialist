## Information Privacy Principles, s 22

All 13 (plus IPP 3A, added later and numbered to sit after IPP 3) sit inside the single section **s 22**. Cite as `s 22 (IPP [number])`.

- **IPP 1 - Purpose of collection.** Personal information must only be collected for a lawful purpose connected with a function or activity of the agency, and collection must be necessary for that purpose.
- **IPP 2 - Source of information.** Personal information should generally be collected directly from the individual concerned, subject to listed exceptions.
- **IPP 3 - Collection directly from the individual: what to tell them.** When collecting directly, the agency must take reasonable steps to ensure the individual is aware of the fact of collection, its purpose, who will hold it, and other specified matters, unless an exception applies.
- **IPP 3A - Collection from someone else.** When collecting from a source other than the individual, the agency must take reasonable steps to tell the individual about the collection, broadly similar to IPP 3's requirements.
- **IPP 4 - Manner of collection.** Personal information must not be collected by unlawful means, or by means that are unfair or unreasonably intrusive, in the circumstances.
- **IPP 5 - Storage and security.** An agency must take reasonable security safeguards against loss, unauthorised access, use, modification, disclosure, or other misuse.
- **IPP 6 - Access.** Individuals have a right to confirm whether an agency holds their personal information and to access it, subject to the withholding grounds in Part 4 of the Act.
- **IPP 7 - Correction.** Individuals have a right to request correction of their personal information, and the agency must take reasonable steps to correct it, or to attach a statement of the requested correction if it does not.
- **IPP 8 - Accuracy.** An agency must not use or disclose personal information without taking reasonable steps to ensure it is accurate, up to date, complete, relevant and not misleading, having regard to the purpose.
- **IPP 9 - Retention.** Personal information must not be kept longer than required for the purposes it may lawfully be used for.
- **IPP 10 - Limits on use.** Personal information collected for one purpose must not be used for another purpose, unless an exception applies (e.g. the individual authorised it, it is directly related to the original purpose, or another listed ground).
- **IPP 11 - Limits on disclosure.** Personal information must not be disclosed to another agency or person, unless an exception applies (similar structure to IPP 10).
- **IPP 12 - Disclosure outside New Zealand.** Before disclosing personal information to a foreign person or entity, the agency must believe on reasonable grounds that the receiving party is subject to comparable privacy safeguards (e.g. bound by the Act itself, a comparable overseas law, a binding scheme, or a contract imposing comparable obligations), or that a specific exception applies (the individual's express authorisation after being expressly informed the protections may not be comparable, or other listed grounds).
- **IPP 13 - Unique identifiers.** An agency may only assign a unique identifier to an individual where necessary for its functions, must not assign the same identifier used by another agency for a different purpose where this would create a risk of confusion, and must not require an individual to disclose a unique identifier unless required or authorised.

## Notifiable privacy breaches, ss 112-122

- **s 112** - defines "notifiable privacy breach": a privacy breach that it is reasonable to believe has caused, or is likely to cause, serious harm to an affected individual.
- **s 113** - factors relevant to assessing the likelihood of serious harm (nature of the information, whether it is protected by security measures, who has obtained it or is likely to, whether it is sensitive, and other specified factors).
- **s 114** - an agency must notify the Privacy Commissioner of a notifiable privacy breach as soon as practicable after becoming aware of it. The Act itself does not state a fixed number of hours; Commissioner guidance commonly references notifying within 72 hours of becoming aware as good practice, but verify the Commissioner's current published guidance before stating a specific figure as a rule, and do not present it as a statutory deadline.
- **s 115** - the agency must also notify affected individuals, or give public notice, as soon as practicable, subject to the exceptions in **s 116** (e.g. notification would be likely to prejudice an investigation, pose a serious threat to health or safety, or other listed grounds, or the Commissioner authorises delay or exception).
- **s 117** - what a notification must contain.
- **s 118** - failing to notify the Commissioner of a notifiable privacy breach is an offence.

## Drafting checks

- A privacy policy clause describing breach response should say the business will assess, notify the Commissioner and affected individuals as required by the Act, and should not promise a fixed response time the Act itself does not guarantee, unless that is the business's own internal commitment (which can be stated as a commitment, clearly separated from the statutory requirement).
- A clause authorising broad internal sharing "across our group of companies" should be checked against IPP 10 and IPP 11: sharing with a related company is still a disclosure/use event requiring a lawful basis, not an automatic exception.
